System

Reader interface

The interface in public/ is a static reader with no build step: one HTML document, a bootstrap module, the modules of public/ui/, one Markdown renderer and a stylesheet per surface. It presents a universe as a book, keeps the reader's own request one link away instead of inside the text, shows work in progress where the next chapter will appear, and opens every secondary surface as a dialog over the page. Interface text is English; the book keeps the language of its universe.

Reading flow

The header is sticky and has two rows. The first row carries the short universe title with a discreet line underneath — the summary of the universe, falling back to its premise, cut to one line — and on the right, in this order, the language selector, the Universes button and a compact ⋯ button. The language selector is filled from GET /api/config, decides the language of the next book, stays available while no universe is loaded and is remembered in the browser under scriptaWorlds.language. Both header buttons stay enabled in every state, because the Edition dialog behind the ⋯ is also the way back to New universe.

The second row is the book navigation: a ‹ control, one number per slice, a › control, the label of the current slice and a counter such as 3/7. The numbers are the chapters of the book plus the turns that are running or queued, each carrying the number of the chapter it will write and marked as a live entry, so a queue of three turns reads as three numbered slices that are on their way. The two controls disable at the ends, and the current number is scrolled into view.

The reading area is a horizontal track in which each chapter is one slide, and a chapter slice contains the chapter and nothing else. Its head is Chapter N · Title followed, on the same row, by the short links i, Rewrite and Console; the request that produced the chapter is never rendered inside the slice, it is read in the dialog behind i, and the agent log unfolds inside the card as the console. Below the head comes the book page: the rendered prose, set in a serif face. Chapter Markdown is rendered into DOM nodes by public/markdown.js, which supports the headings, paragraphs, blockquotes, scene breaks and emphasis that the narrative contract allows and never injects markup from chapter text. Prose is fetched lazily through GET /api/universes/:id/chapters/:number and cached in the browser by size and creation time.

Work in progress is part of the same track, as numbered slices. A queued turn shows a waiting line with its one-based position in that universe's queue — Waiting (1 in queue) means nothing is ahead of it; a running turn shows Writing chapter N… with the current phase and the tail of the live text, while the tool calls of the agent stay out of the flow and are read in the console of the chapter once it exists. A turn that failed or was interrupted shows its message and a retry action. When a new chapter arrives while the reader is not at the end of the book, a discreet New chapter ready — read it control offers to move there instead of pulling the reader away.

Starting a book: Custom, Library and Ingredients

When no universe is selected, the reading area becomes the start screen. It opens with one short paragraph under an information glyph that says what a template is and that nothing starts before Send, then four tabs, and it holds no form field of its own except the field of the Custom tab and the file field of the Import tab. Custom is open first and is the fastest way in for a reader who arrives with an idea: one large text area for the specification of the world — what exists, what is impossible, who is in it, what is already wrong — and one Start button, which creates the universe with that text as its fundamental law and begins its first chapter from the same text, exactly as the empty template composes a world. A specification shorter than a sentence is refused in the panel before anything is sent, and the answer of the server appears under the same field without clearing the text. Library lists the start templates served by GET /api/library in a column on the left — title, sector, genealogy, the one-line summary of the problem at the centre of that world and the symbols of the cells it activates — and reads the chosen template through GET /api/library/:slug into a second column on the right: the request text the template sends to ALA, a Use control that puts that template in charge of the request field — it turns into In use and disables itself while the template holds the field — then every indication the book gives for it as a labelled list, then the source text of the book in a folded block. Opening a row only reads a template; the field is rewritten when the reader presses Use. Ingredients shows the table of ideas served by GET /api/table: twelve families as rows and fifteen operators as columns, with a search box, a legend of the operators, a counter of the form N of 6 chosen, one chip per chosen ingredient with a control that removes it, a control that clears the selection, and a fold that adds an element the table does not contain, with a name and an optional family, operator and note. A cell, an operator name or a family name opens the Details dialog.

The Details dialog of a cell shows the idea in one line, the longer prompt when it differs, what that family changes, the work the cell is Seen in and the worlds that already use it — the label counts them — each with its title, sector, situation and problem, and a control that opens that template in the Library tab. It offers Insert in my new world to put the cell into the compound, the control turning into In your world and disabling itself once the cell is chosen. Of an operator or a family it shows the mechanism or the family instead, with the cells that belong to it.

The Library and Ingredients tabs compose one text instead of excluding each other: the field holds the request of the chosen template followed by an Operations added by me: block listing the chosen operations, and each of them is written with the ingredient sentence of its cell — a sentence, never the two-letter address that identifies the cell in the table. A template therefore stays chosen while the reader browses the table, ingredients stay chosen while the reader browses the library, and every change in either tab writes the composed text back into the request field.

Import takes a book the team did not write here: one file field for a PDF or a DOCX, an optional title and an optional language, and one Upload the book button. The upload carries the file itself, the server hashes and bounds it while it streams in, and the panel then shows the import record in the server's own words — the file name and format, what the reader is told about the reading state, the title it detected and whether that title came out of the file or from the uploaded name, the chapter and word count, and every warning the extraction produced. When the text is read, Create the universe creates the book and starts its first import turn, and the button becomes Continue the import while chapters are still to come, carrying one bounded window after another until the book reports itself complete, at which point the interface opens the new universe. A file whose text cannot be read — a scan without a text layer, a damaged package — is refused with the server's code and message in the panel, and the reader may choose another file. The same panel stops if a turn adds no chapter, and says which universe to open to read the reason instead of queueing the same window again.

The request field of the composer is the input for everything that follows, and it is sent with Send; the large field of the Custom tab is a second input that creates a world directly with Start. Nothing is created while the reader browses the tabs: pressing Send sends one POST /api/universes carrying the reader's text as prompt, the language of the header, start: true, and both library and elements when a template and extra operations were chosen. The server composes one universe from them — the cells of the template first, the chosen ingredients added on top, deduplicated and cut at six — and queues its first chapter from the same text. Text alone composes the world: a request without a template and without an ingredient becomes the law of the new universe, the same way the empty start template composes one from the reader's request, so pressing Send on a sentence the reader wrote always starts a book, and pressing Start in the Custom tab is the same composition with the field of that tab as the text. A refusal is shown next to the field that caused it, which is where BAD_LAW, BAD_TEMPLATE, UNKNOWN_TEMPLATE, BAD_ELEMENTS (with the message Six ingredients are the most a universe law can hold. Remove one first.), UNKNOWN_ELEMENT, BAD_ELEMENT_FAMILY and BAD_ELEMENT_OPERATOR appear, and where a too-short law is reported as BAD_LAW.

Composer and offers

Below the book, one sticky card holds ALA's voice and the [composer](wiki.html#definition-composer) together. When the current chapter has an offer, the card shows its teaser, clamped to two lines with an inline more and less control when it is longer, and up to three compact links with the decisions of that offer. Pressing one fills the field with the prompt it carries and focuses it; it does not send anything, so the reader edits the text before sending it. When a chapter has no offer, the card shows the first continuation idea derived from the universe's threads as a suggestion that fills the field instead of sending it.

The request field accepts anything and grows with its content up to seven lines — or a little under half of the window height on a short screen — and then scrolls inside itself. It can also be made taller by hand: a small handle in its top right corner, marked ⋰, is dragged upwards, which grows the field because the composer sits at the bottom of the page, and the height the reader chose is theirs from then on, since the automatic growth only ever raises the lower bound. The textarea itself carries no native resize corner, and the handle is hidden on small screens, where the field simply grows with the text. The field sends on Enter, inserts a newline on Shift+Enter, and keeps its text when a request cannot be sent. With no universe loaded its placeholder invites the reader to pick a universe or start a new one and the typed text is preserved. Every request is submitted as a normal turn, so it joins the same durable queue as every other request and is never refused because the universe is busy.

Dialogs

Nothing unfolds in the reading flow. Every secondary surface is a fixed overlay drawn above the page over a dimmed backdrop, with a visible ×, centred on both axes, closed on Esc, on a click on the backdrop and through the ×. There are eight of them: the four large ones — Universes, What you asked for, Rewrite chapter and Details — are nearly full screen (width: min(96vw, 1200px), max-height: 92vh with their own scrolling), the Review, Reports and reviews and Reader feedback dialogs use the analysis panel (width: min(96vw, 1000px)), and the Edition dialog is deliberately small. Reading is never a dialog: a chapter always occupies the page.

Dialog Opened from Content
Universes The header button The universes on disk, most recently updated first, with closed ones grouped at the end under Closed; each row shows the title, the language label and the chapter count. Selecting a row loads that universe and closes the dialog, and the dialog never opens by itself.
Edition The compact ⋯ button Download PDF and Download DOCX, shown as not yet and disabled until the file exists, then Generate edition with its queued or working state, a separator and New universe, which returns to the start screen. There is no close or reopen action in the interface.
What you asked for The i link of a chapter The request of the turn that wrote that chapter, read from the turn record when the universe detail does not already carry it, and the chapter it belongs to.
Details A cell, an operator name or a family name in the Ingredients grid What stands behind one entry of the table. For a cell it reads GET /api/table/cells/:symbol and shows the idea in one line with the longer prompt under it, what that family changes, the work the cell is Seen in, and the worlds that already use it — the heading counts them — each with its title, sector, situation, problem and a control that opens that template in the Library tab. The action is Insert in my new world, which becomes In your world and is disabled once the cell is chosen; a chosen cell is removed from the × of its chip in the selection, not from here. For an operator or a family it describes the mechanism or the family and lists the cells that work that way. It is opened from the start screen rather than from a chapter.
Rewrite chapter The pencil icon of a chapter's toolbar What is wrong and what should change, with the consequence of the rewrite stated before it is confirmed.
Review The review icon of a chapter's toolbar, or the reports icon Starting a review and watching it: the phase (measurement or continuity), the scope (this chapter, an arc of chapters or the whole book), who produces the observations (the configured evaluator, or nobody for measurements only), and an optional aggregate with the stated intention of the author. The panel follows the run the server records — queued, running, done, or the error it recorded — and offers Retry on a failure.
Reports and reviews The reports icon of a chapter's toolbar The runs of that book, newest first, each with its frozen version, scope, trigger, who supplied the observations, and a historical label when its version is no longer the accepted one. Opening one shows the bundle: the metrics with a reason in place of every number the review could not produce, the indicators, the findings with their severity, certainty and frozen quotations, the passages the report marks as worth preserving, and the five published views rendered from their own markdown. Quotations are read from the frozen bundle, never from the current prose.
Reader feedback The feedback icon of a chapter's toolbar The reader's own answer and what the team said, in two panes behind one strip. The pane a reader answers in holds the frozen copy the questions are about, the questionnaire with its anchors, its skip control and its two optional reactions, the comments with their quotations and the identity the host issued for this book; the second pane lists the book's responses, each with who gave it, which copy it answers and in which state it stands. A Compare two versions control in the same dialog opens the comparison and its two-text reading session.

Live updates

The client keeps one state object and rebuilds the affected surfaces from it: the universe detail supplies the book structure, a chapter cache supplies prose, and a live map supplies the state of unfinished turns. Live updates come from the universe event stream, which is the same mechanism a chapter uses to publish its progress to every open reader.

A page reload during a turn loses nothing: the universe detail carries the running and waiting jobs, so the client reconstructs the live state and resubscribes. If the stream cannot be established, or keeps failing, the client opens one stream per job and polls the job route every 1.8 seconds, which turns live text into slower progress rather than into a broken page.

Rewriting and editions

A pending turn is numbered by the chapter it will write, counted in chapters from the accepted book and never in turn numbers, so a queue of three requests behind a book of eight chapters reads 9, 10 and 11 even after rewrites and editions have pushed the turn numbers ahead, and an edition never takes a chapter number. While a turn is writing or replacing a chapter, that chapter leaves the accepted list and the reader sees the live slice in its place; the chapter returns by itself when the turn commits, or when a failure restores it. The bottom card also says what sending does: on any chapter that is not the last one, a hint states that the request opens the next chapter instead of changing what is on screen, and points at Rewrite for that. The Rewrite link on a chapter opens the rewrite dialog, which asks what is wrong and what should change. A submission shorter than a sentence is rejected in the dialog. When the server answers 409 LATER_CHAPTERS, the dialog states which chapters would be removed and turns its submit control into an explicit confirmation that resends with dropLater: true, which is the only way the interface ever asks to discard chapters; the superseded versions stay in the book's archive.

A chapter's actions are icon buttons in one toolbar beside its title: what you asked for, rewrite, the scene log, review, and reports and reviews. Each carries an accessible name and a tooltip, the two that depend on a turn record appear only when it exists, and the scene log control reports whether it is expanded. The same toolbar is how a reader reaches a review of that chapter or of the whole book, and how the reports of the book are opened.

Editions stay out of the reading flow: they are reached only from the Edition dialog, they are never reading pages and they never appear as slices. While an export turn is queued or running the dialog shows that state instead of the generation action, and when the turn finishes the universe is reloaded and the download links appear. Downloads are plain links, so the browser handles the transfer.

Closing a universe is not offered by the interface: POST /api/universes/:id/close and POST /api/universes/:id/open stay available to API clients, and the reading interface only offers New universe, which returns to the start screen. A universe that was closed elsewhere keeps its last chapter readable, and a new chapter for it is refused by the server with CLOSED.

Persisted state, keyboard and errors

The current universe and the reading position are kept in localStorage under keys prefixed with scriptaWorlds. and mirrored into the URL query, so a link reopens the same chapter and a stored universe is used when the URL does not name one. A browser that refuses storage degrades to URL-only state instead of failing. Arrow keys move between slides unless focus is in a text field or a modifier key is held, Escape closes an open dialog, and a horizontal swipe over the reading area moves between slides with a threshold of roughly a seventh of the width, bounded between 44 and 90 pixels, so that vertical scrolling still works.

Visible errors are deduplicated, limited to a few, appended to the current slide with a dismiss control, and never replace the book. Error text is short and English, and the interface relies on the error code when a failure needs a specific reaction, as with a confirmed rewrite.

Language rules

Interface strings, button labels, placeholders and error messages are English, and the document declares lang="en". The book is not translated: chapter prose is rendered inside an element whose lang attribute is the universe language, and the header selector shows the codes reported by the server. A chapter's offer and its teaser come from the agent and are written in the language of the book, so the composer mixes English chrome with the voice of the universe on purpose. The interface has no surface for changing a book's language; it is chosen in the header before the book is created and can be changed through the API. The surfaces, rules and fallbacks are specified in DS006-reader-interface.

Modules

The interface is plain ES modules with no build step and no framework. public/app.js is the bootstrap: it binds the DOM, wires the events, loads the configuration and the universes, restores the reading position and starts the reader. Everything else lives in public/ui/ — state.js, api.js, errors.js, universe.js, slides.js, overlays.js, actions.js and sse.js — with one module per surface under public/ui/render/ (header.js, composer.js, menu.js, reader.js, explore.js, library.js, ingredients.js, tableinfo.js), and the stylesheets are split the same way under public/styles/ (base.css, reader.css, live.css, explore.css, composer.css, overlays.css). Every one of those files stays under 500 lines. A module that fails to load — typically because the interface was replaced while a tab stayed open — is reported by a Reload banner instead of leaving a dead page; the banner is described on Operations.

The analysis surface

Reviewing a book is separate from reading it: the work happens outside the store, over a frozen copy of one accepted version, and the interface only shows what the server recorded. public/ui/review.js holds the review state and the actions that start a run, follow it and retry it; public/ui/report.js holds the report state, loads the bundle and its five views and keeps which view is open. Their surfaces are rendered by public/ui/render/review.js, public/ui/render/report.js, public/ui/render/bundle.js for the bundle's own sections and public/ui/render/runs.js for the list of runs. public/ui/icons.js holds the inline SVG icons the toolbar and the panels use, so no icon font, image file or external request is involved.

The panels poll while a run is unsettled and stop when it settles, and the universe event stream asks them to refresh when a turn finishes, so a review started in one tab appears in another without a reload. A metric the review could not produce is never shown as a zero: the panel prints the reason the bundle records in its place.

Reader feedback

Reader feedback is where the reader says what they think of the book they are reading. The feedback icon in the toolbar of a chapter slice opens it, and inside it two panes sit behind one strip — the one the reader answers in and the one that reads back what the team said. Nothing in it is averaged and no answer is scored, and the interface is the same for every reader because the API has no accounts: what one person's answers are attributed to is a reader-identity.v1 record the server issued for this book and this browser remembered, never an account that unlocks anything.

Answering begins by freezing the text. Opening the pane reads the accepted version from the listing and freezes it as a target outside universes/, sending that version together with the sha256 of every chapter this browser displayed, so the response is anchored to the copy the reader actually saw rather than to the book as it stands when the form is submitted. The panel says what was frozen — the scope, the version and the questionnaire version — and a rewrite that landed between reading and answering is answered with STALE_TARGET: nothing is frozen then, every answer and comment stays in the form, and the control that reports it reads the accepted text again and freezes that instead. When the frozen copy is an earlier version the panel says so, a response to it is listed as historical, and the reader may freeze the version now on screen; the questions never change under a reader, because each target carries the questionnaire it was frozen with, and any question may be skipped, a skipped answer being recorded as skipped and never read as a low one.

The pane quotes the frozen copy and not the book behind it. A chapter this browser no longer holds, or one whose text is not the one the target recorded, is read back from the target's own frozen bytes through GET /api/universes/:id/feedback/targets/:targetId/text/:name and shown as the prose the reader was given, so a passage selected in the dialog resolves inside that copy and a reader on another machine quotes the same words. Everything one reading session adds belongs to that reader's own response and to no other: the two optional declarations of prior exposure — the model's scores or findings, and other readers' comments — are recorded exactly as declared and never assumed on the reader's behalf, and the two optional reactions the questionnaire declares, usefulness about the report that was open and defect_present about the text itself, each carry their own optional sentence and stay undeclared when the reader leaves them untouched. The place a reader read is asked for and never required, and the identity is remembered in this browser, so the same person is not asked twice and a rename keeps every response already given attributed to them.

A Compare two versions control in the same pane opens the comparison session: the reader picks two of the book's frozen readings and a question both declare, and the session is the observation they record on purpose — each side shown from the frozen bytes of its own target, in the place order the session drew once and stored, so an earlier version is read as the text its reader was given, and the two sides referred to by place and by role. Other readers' answers are the second pane of the same dialog and are shown after the reader has answered: the store records who answered and what they declared they had already seen, and it claims nothing about who read the book before answering.
