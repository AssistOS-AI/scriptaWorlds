scripta-story-design

scripta-story-design prepares a book, an arc or a deliberate change of direction before any prose is written. It turns the permanent law, the reader request and the accepted chapters into a compact design brief that gives the next episode a purpose inside the book, and it ships one dependency-free validator for that brief. It is a separate-phase skill: it is chosen for design work and never runs during chapter writing.

What the skill covers

A chapter prompt can ask for the next chapter; it cannot hold the decisions that make a sequence of chapters into a book. The skill carries those decisions: what situation or possibility the story is asking about, what premise makes it actionable by connecting a person, a want, resistance and a consequence, which question stays open while characters reach incompatible answers, and what experience the reader was promised together with its meaningful uncertainty.

It also carries the people and the world. A character direction records a private want, a contradiction, a habitual defence and the limits of that character's knowledge, and is kept apart from an observed attribute. A relationship direction records who wants what to change without overwriting the accepted relationship. A world assumption records its epistemic kind — a permanent law, an observation, a character belief, a social rule, an unresolved hypothesis or a plan — so that a theory a character holds is never silently promoted into the physics of the universe.

The folder contains SKILL.md with the working order and the schema, references/concepts.md with the working definitions of idea, premise, theme, structure and blueprint, references/design-workflow.md with the full workflow, references/source-map.md with the provenance of the adapted definitions, skill.json as the catalog manifest, dependencies.md as the local dependency record, and scripts/validate-design.mjs with its rule module in scripts/lib/design.mjs and its packet loader in scripts/lib/packet.mjs.

The design brief

The brief is one JSON document, proposal/story-design.json in an assessment workspace, with the schema version design.v1. Its required keys are the schema version, an identifier, the language of the book, the central idea, the premise, the thematic question and the reader promise. Everything else is optional: the structural intent, the character and relationship directions, the world assumptions, the arcs and the open design questions.

Each arc carries an identifier, the pressure it applies, possible destinations and a planning status of planned, active, completed or abandoned, and it may carry known chapter memberships and the reader decision points that can change its route. Chapter memberships are positive chapter numbers: a negative or zero number is refused, while a number above the last accepted chapter is a planned future membership and stays legal. A completed status written here is an intention and not an accepted event — an arc with no accepted members cannot stand as an accepted event — so the validator warns about it, exactly as it warns about a plan that describes a future event. A sparse brief is legal, and an empty arc list is valid: the workflow prefers a recorded gap to an invented certainty, and a planned ending can be replaced without rewriting the history that the accepted prose recorded.

The working order is fixed: read the permanent law and the reader request and separate the explicit constraints from the assumptions the author may explore; write the idea, the premise and the question; choose the focal character and the people whose wants complicate that character's task; sketch the first arc as a direction with possible turns, the cost of failure and at least one possible relationship change; identify which reader interventions can alter a consequential route, with the permanent law outside that freedom; and then write the brief and validate it.

Validator

The skill ships one read-only command:

node skills/scripta-story-design/scripts/validate-design.mjs --input <design.json> [--context <packet-dir|manifest.json>]

The command prints exactly one JSON object with schema_version, ok, errors and warnings, and it exits with status 0 for a valid brief, 2 for invalid arguments, malformed input, a refused packet or a violated schema or reference rule, and 1 for an unexpected execution failure. It checks the required keys and their types, unique identifiers including collisions after trimming or Unicode normalization, every entity reference, the enumerated epistemic kinds and arc statuses, positive chapter memberships, the supported language code, and — when a context packet is given — the packet itself and the version agreement described below. It has no --out: the command writes nothing, so no publication step can leave a partial result behind. A malformed document is answered with a structured error list rather than a stack trace, and input files are never modified.

A plan-kind world assumption that mentions a future event is reported as a warning rather than an error, because a plan is exactly the place where a future event may be written down without becoming canon, and an arc whose status is completed is warned about for the same reason: a brief is a proposal, so the status records an intention rather than an accepted event. The validator never certifies that the answers are good literature; the editorial questions that follow it are whether the brief identifies a dramatizable human pressure, leaves room for ordinary life beyond explaining the law, keeps a planned surprise compatible with established evidence, allows the next reader decision to change a route, and chooses a structural form because it suits this book, including deliberate ambiguity or deliberate stability.

The frozen packet behind --context

--context names an assessment packet — the packet directory that contains manifest.json, or that manifest file itself. The manifest must be an assessment-input.v2 document, and it is verified before the brief is judged: the schema version (a superseded assessment-input.v1 manifest is refused explicitly, because it carries no accepted version), every declared path (relative, forward slashes, no .., and still inside the packet after symlinks are resolved through real paths), a duplicate path or artifact identifier (DUPLICATE_PATH, DUPLICATE_ARTIFACT_ID), a role outside the documented vocabulary (ROLE_UNDECLARED), a chapter number declared twice, a missing or unreadable file, a byte count or SHA-256 that differs, bytes that are not valid UTF-8 (INVALID_ENCODING), and the accepted version recomputed from the chapter, offer, canon, threads and atlas entries and compared with the declared version. The declared scope.kind is enforced with the packet: complete must carry every chapter from 1 to the last accepted chapter plus the canon, threads and atlas roles; partial must declare the chapters it omits; textual_only carries prose and no state container.

The brief's based_on_version is the accepted content identity of §8.2, and it is compared with the packet's version — never with a universe id and never with a capture time. A brief written for version A of a book is therefore refused as stale against version B of the same book and accepted for B, and a proposal whose version is no longer accepted is reported as stale rather than applied. Without --context a brief that names a source version is answered with a warning, so an unchecked version never looks like agreement.

Where a proposal lives and how it travels

A brief is written to proposal/story-design.json inside the external assessment workspace of docs/contracts.md §8.1, never into a universe folder, and the frozen input/ packet is read-only for the whole phase. A proposal is not evidence: the direction it records is not part of the book until an accepted chapter shows it, and the skill writes no chapter and touches neither the canon, the threads nor the atlas.

The only bridge to a later writing turn is an explicit human approval: the approval record carries the proposal's path and hash, the accepted version it was written against, the decision, the reviewer, the timestamp and the concrete accepted directions. At a later authoring request the host selects the relevant approved directions and passes them as input data of that request; the chapter prompt names scripta-ala and never asks the agent to read this skill, one of its references or a handbook while writing. Declined directions stay declined and are not proposed again.

Dependencies and boundaries

The folder needs Node.js 20 or later and nothing else: the validator uses built-in modules for file access, paths and hashing, and it has no npm package, no vendored code, no external command and no network access at runtime. Its tests use the built-in node --test runner against temporary directories outside the repository, and they cover a minimal brief, a full brief, an intentionally non-linear or static design, duplicate identifiers including normalized collisions, an undeclared entity reference, malformed input, a missing required key, an empty arc list, malformed collection entries, an unsupported language, a document of another kind such as the metrics-side evaluation configuration, a plan-kind assumption and a completed arc, invalid negative chapter memberships beside a legitimate future plan, the requirement that the input file is byte-identical after a run, and the packet cases: stale and accepted versions of the same book, tampered bytes, symlink escape, traversal, duplicate identifiers, a superseded manifest, an undeclared role, non-UTF-8 bytes and every declared scope.

The skill owns design intent, not accepted narrative state. It does not write a chapter, does not update the canon, the threads or the atlas, and does not change the permanent law or the charter, which belong to the server and to a human. A future event becomes part of the book only when accepted prose establishes it. The complete contract is specified in DS010-scripta-story-design-skill; the craft decisions that follow a design are scripta-prose-craft, which owns the prose profile, while an evaluation configuration belongs to scripta-metrics-report. This skill defines neither block and shares no schema identifier with them.
