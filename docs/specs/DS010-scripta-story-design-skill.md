## Introduction

`skills/scripta-story-design/` prepares a book, an arc or a deliberate change of direction before any prose is written. Its output is a compact design brief: a central idea, a premise, an open thematic question, the experience promised to the reader, the characters whose incompatible wants make that question matter, the relationships that can change, the world assumptions with their epistemic kind, and flexible arcs with possible destinations. It ships one executable, `scripts/validate-design.mjs`, which checks the structure, the identifiers and the references of that brief and verifies the frozen `assessment-input.v2` packet it was written against. This specification states the brief schema, the command-line contract, the packet and accepted-version rules, the working order and the editorial questions the skill prescribes, the approved transfer that carries a proposal into a later writing request, and the boundary that keeps the skill out of the chapter-writing turn. The narrative skill that writes a chapter is `DS007-scripta-ala-skill`, the draft files a book uses are in `DS004-universe-storage`, and the prose decisions that follow a design belong to `DS011-scripta-prose-craft-skill`.

## Core Content

### Purpose and scope

A book project needs decisions that no single chapter prompt can hold: what the story is asking, which experience the reader was promised, whose wants collide, and which routes a reader intervention may still open. This skill carries those decisions as one small, revisable document, so a later chapter can be given a purpose inside the book instead of being asked to invent one from the last chapter alone.

The skill is a separate-phase skill. It runs when a design is prepared or revised, not while a chapter is being written, and nothing in this specification adds a step to the chapter turn. Its brief is tentative by design: a planned ending can be replaced without rewriting history, and the accepted prose is always the evidence for what actually happened.

The folder contains `SKILL.md` with the working order and the schema, `references/concepts.md` with the working definitions of idea, premise, theme, structure, blueprint, character and world assumption, `references/design-workflow.md` with the full workflow, the approval transfer and the proposed artifact, `references/source-map.md` with the provenance of the adapted definitions, `skill.json` as the catalog manifest, `dependencies.md` as the local dependency record, `scripts/validate-design.mjs` as the single executable, `scripts/lib/design.mjs` with the brief's validation rules, and `scripts/lib/packet.mjs` with the `assessment-input.v2` packet loader of §8.3.

### Design brief

The brief lives in the `proposal/` directory of an external assessment workspace, as `story-design.json`, and uses the schema version `design.v1`; it is never written into a universe folder, because a design is a proposal rather than accepted narrative state (`docs/contracts.md` §8). Narrative strings are written in the language of the book; keys and enumerated values stay English.

Required keys are `schema_version` with the value `design.v1`, `design_id`, `language`, `central_idea`, `premise`, `thematic_question` and `reader_promise`. A central idea is the situation or possibility that can sustain the inquiry; a premise makes it actionable by connecting a person, a want, resistance and a consequence; a thematic question states what the story examines and stays open while characters reach incompatible answers; a reader promise names an expected experience together with a meaningful uncertainty.

Optional keys are `based_on_version`, `structural_intent`, `character_directions`, `relationship_directions`, `world_assumptions`, `arcs` and `open_design_questions`. `character_directions` entries require `entity_id`, `want` and `contradiction`, and accept `defence`, `knowledge_limits` and `direction`; they describe a desired future development and are kept apart from observed attributes. `relationship_directions` entries require `participants`, `terms`, `last_change` and `evidence`, and the participants are at least two distinct entity references that must resolve to a declared entity whenever the brief declares entities at all. `world_assumptions` entries require `id`, `epistemic_kind`, `text` and `source`, where the epistemic kind is one of `law`, `observation`, `belief`, `social_rule`, `hypothesis` or `plan`: a law is the permanent constraint, an observation is something seen to happen, a belief belongs to a character, a social rule is an institution, a hypothesis stays unresolved, and a plan is a future intention that is never evidence that an event occurred. `arcs` entries require `id`, `pressure`, `destinations` and `status`, where the status is one of `planned`, `active`, `completed` or `abandoned`, and accept `chapter_memberships`, `decision_points` and `planning_status`; a chapter membership is a positive integer, a membership above the last accepted chapter is a planned future membership and stays legal, and a `completed` status written in a brief is an intention, because only accepted prose establishes that an arc completed.

Identifiers are compared in one identity form — surrounding whitespace trimmed and Unicode NFC normalization applied — so two identifiers that differ only by that are the same identifier and collide; duplicates are refused within arcs, within entities, within world assumptions and between `design_id` and any of those. The `language` key must be one of the supported fiction languages, and an unsupported code is refused. `based_on_version` is the accepted content identity of `docs/contracts.md` §8.2, never a universe id and never a timestamp.

Sparse briefs are legal. An empty `arcs` array, an absent `structural_intent` and a brief with only the required keys are all valid, because an invented certainty is worse than a recorded gap. The working definitions behind these fields are in `references/concepts.md`, and the workflow that produces them is in `references/design-workflow.md`.

### Validator contract

```sh
node skills/scripta-story-design/scripts/validate-design.mjs \
  --input <design.json> [--context <packet-dir|manifest.json>]
```

`--input` names the brief and is required; `--out` does not exist, because this command writes nothing at all. It never creates, rewrites or deletes a file, never touches a universe and derives no default path from a repository root.

`--context` optionally names a frozen assessment packet, given either as the packet directory that contains `manifest.json` or as that manifest file itself. The packet must be an `assessment-input.v2` manifest (`docs/contracts.md` §8.3), and the loader — `scripts/lib/packet.mjs`, duplicated in every consumer so each skill stands alone — refuses it before the brief is judged. The structured refusals are a missing context path (`MISSING_CONTEXT`), a missing or unreadable manifest (`MISSING_MANIFEST`), unparsable JSON (`BAD_JSON`), a manifest that is not an object or that violates the shape of §8.3 (`INVALID_MANIFEST`), an unknown `schema_version`, with an explicit refusal for the superseded `assessment-input.v1` (`SCHEMA_VERSION`), an absolute path, a `..` component or a path that leaves the packet after symlinks are resolved through real paths (`PATH_ESCAPE`), a duplicate `path` (`DUPLICATE_PATH`), a duplicate `artifact_id` (`DUPLICATE_ARTIFACT_ID`), a `role` outside the documented vocabulary (`ROLE_UNDECLARED`), a chapter number declared twice (`DUPLICATE_CHAPTER`), a declared file that does not exist or cannot be read (`MISSING_FILE`), a byte count that differs (`BYTE_MISMATCH`), a SHA-256 that differs (`HASH_MISMATCH`), bytes that are not valid UTF-8 (`INVALID_ENCODING`), a declared `version` that differs from the recomputed one (`VERSION_MISMATCH`), and a scope that contradicts the packet (`SCOPE_INCOMPLETE`, `SCOPE_INCONSISTENT`).

Only the files whose role is `chapter`, `offer`, `canon`, `threads` or `atlas` take part in the accepted-version identity, which is recomputed as `sha256:<hex>` over `` `${path}\t${sha256}\t${bytes}\n` `` entries sorted by path in byte order (§8.2); `meta` is excluded because the server rewrites `universe.json` on every turn, and `design`, `profile`, `annotations`, `corpus`, `rules` and `timing` are inputs to an assessment rather than accepted narrative content. `scope.kind` is the declared honesty of the packet: `complete` requires every chapter from 1 to `book.last_accepted_chapter` together with the `canon`, `threads` and `atlas` roles, `partial` declares the chapters it contains in `scope.chapters` and the rest in `scope.omitted` so that a missing interior chapter is a declared omission rather than an error, and `textual_only` carries chapter prose and no state container. The `based_on_version` of the brief is then compared with the packet's `version`, never with `book.universe_id`: a brief written for version A of a book is refused as stale (`STALE_BASED_ON_VERSION`) against version B of the same book, and accepted for B. Without `--context`, a brief that names a `based_on_version` is answered with a warning rather than with silence, because an unchecked version must never look like agreement.

Standard output carries exactly one JSON object, `{ "schema_version": "design.v1", "ok": boolean, "errors": [], "warnings": [] }`, and standard error carries human diagnostics. Exit status `0` means a valid brief, `2` means an invalid argument, a malformed document, a packet refusal or a violated schema or reference rule, and `1` means an unexpected execution failure. A malformed JSON document is answered with a structured error list and exit status `2`, never with a stack trace.

The validator checks what a machine can settle: the required keys and their types; unique `design_id`, arc identifiers, entity identifiers and assumption identifiers, compared in the identity form described above; every entity reference declared by a character direction or a world assumption, including a relationship participant that resolves to nothing; the enumerated `epistemic_kind` and arc `status` values; positive integer chapter memberships; the supported language code; and, with `--context`, the whole packet and version agreement described above. A `plan`-kind world assumption that mentions a future event is reported as a warning, because a plan is exactly the place where a future event may be written down without becoming canon, and an arc whose status is `completed` is reported as a proposal for the same reason. A legitimate future arc plan — a membership above the last accepted chapter, or a planned arc that has not started — is not an error.

### Workspace, separation and transfer

A design brief lives in the `proposal/` directory of the external assessment workspace of `docs/contracts.md` §8.1, `<workspace>/<universe-id>/<accepted-version>/<run-id>/proposal/story-design.json`, where `<accepted-version>` is the §8.2 identity with `:` replaced by `-`. The workspace root is chosen by the operator and stated explicitly on the command line; a standalone user may supply any authorized equivalent layout. Nothing in it is narrative state: a design phase writes only inside its own run directory, never into `universes/`, and never mutates the frozen `input/` packet it reads.

A proposal is never evidence. A planned event, an arc destination or a direction recorded in a proposal is not part of the book until an accepted chapter shows it, and this skill neither writes a chapter, nor updates `canon.md`, `threads.json` or `atlas.json`, nor changes the permanent law or the charter. The proposal records the exact packet `version` it was written against (`based_on_version`); when the accepted version of that book has moved on, the proposal is stale and is reported as stale rather than applied, and an approval whose proposal changed cannot be applied silently either.

The only bridge to a later writing turn is the explicit human approval of §8.4: `approval.json` carries the proposal's path and hash, the accepted version it was written against, the decision of `approved` or `declined`, the reviewer, the timestamp and the concrete accepted directions. At a later authoring request the host selects the relevant approved directions and passes them as **input data** of that request; the chapter prompt names `scripta-ala` (and `scripta-book-export` for an edition) and never asks the agent to read this skill, one of its references, or a handbook mid-chapter. Declined directions stay declined and are not proposed again. This mechanical transfer is what keeps a prose request carrying approved design intent while a writing turn stays free of any design or review skill.

### Working order

The prescribed order is to read the permanent law and the reader request first and to separate explicit constraints from assumptions the author is free to explore; then to write the central idea, the premise and the thematic question; then to choose the first focal character and the people whose wants complicate that character's task; then to sketch the initial arc as a direction with possible turns, the cost of failure and at least one possible relationship change; then to identify the reader interventions that can alter a consequential route, with the permanent law outside that freedom unless a human changes it; and finally to write the brief and validate it.

The editorial questions that follow the validator are not mechanical. A useful brief identifies a human pressure that can be dramatized, leaves room for ordinary life beyond explaining the world law, keeps a planned surprise compatible with established evidence, allows the next reader decision to change a route, and chooses a structural form because it suits this book, including deliberate ambiguity or deliberate stability. The validator never certifies that these answers are good literature, and a deliberate non-linear, static or quiet design is not a defect to be corrected.

### Dependencies and boundary

The skill requires Node.js 20 or later and uses `node:fs/promises`, `node:path` and `node:crypto` only. It has no npm package, no vendored code, no external command and no network access at runtime, and its `dependencies.md` records that position. Its tests run with the built-in `node --test` runner and `node:assert/strict` against temporary directories outside the repository, and they cover a minimal new-book brief, a full brief with arcs, characters, relationships and world assumptions, an intentionally non-linear or static design, duplicate identifiers including normalized identity collisions, an undeclared entity reference, malformed input, a missing required key, an empty arc list, malformed collection entries, an unsupported language, a document of another kind including the metrics-side evaluation configuration, a plan-kind assumption that mentions a future event, an arc whose status is `completed`, invalid negative chapter memberships alongside a legitimate future arc plan, the byte-identical input requirement, and the packet cases: a brief accepted for the version it names and refused as stale against another version of the same book, tampered hashes and byte counts, a manifest that declares another version, a missing manifest and a manifest detached from its files, a path escaping the packet through a symlink, an absolute and a traversal path, a duplicate artifact identifier, a superseded `assessment-input.v1` manifest, an undeclared role and bytes that are not valid UTF-8, the declared scope of a complete, partial, textual-only and state-poor packet, and the explicit warning when `based_on_version` could not be checked.

The boundary is that this skill owns design intent, not accepted narrative state. It does not write a chapter, does not update the canon, the thread list or the atlas, and does not change the permanent law or the charter, which remain the server's and the human's documents. It has no publication step: the command validates a document and writes nothing, so a proposal reaches the book only through the approved transfer described above. A future event becomes part of the book only when accepted prose establishes it, and the design brief may be revised or abandoned without rewriting the history that the prose recorded. A prose profile belongs to `DS011-scripta-prose-craft-skill` (`profile.v1`) and an evaluation configuration to the metrics skill (`assessment-profile.v1`); this skill defines neither and shares no schema identifier with them.
