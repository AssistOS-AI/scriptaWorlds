# Plan chapter 0005

- dramatic_question: When the officer writes an adult wording of the sign into the green journal over the Reader's half reading, does the plant answer the writing or the act done under it?
- anchor_character: Officer Rusk.
- character_want: To have a page he can pay before the count takes his eye on the third morning, and to keep the book the thing that tells the farm what to do.
- primary_idea: The book is a channel from the officer to the crew and never to the plant, so a wording the farm can always perform ends the count without the plant hearing a word (Atlas: green-journal / writing-unheard).
- human_need: Meaning and control — an officer's need to be the man who answers, not the man who waits.
- opening_hook: Rusk is at the shelf before the first shift with the pen already wet, and the count on his third line has one morning left in it.
- beats:
  - Rusk writes the adult wording over Meren's objection and in front of Bea, reading the one part of the sign the whole farm agrees on: the long stroke, blood over the roots until the plant is still.
  - He opens his own palm over the bare soil of the root bed; the crown, the low leaves and the open flower turn down to the wet ground under his hand, and the ring reads the turn as the plant accepting the blood.
  - Four crew hands follow him over the soil; Meren bandages every cut at once because the plant has not shaken for four days, so the old rite has no clock left and a cut buys nothing but a scar.
  - Bea and Meren say out loud in front of the crew that the plant turned the same way for a hand lamp in chapter 3, and the ring hears it and keeps the old certainty.
  - The woman from the second shift refuses to cut, says the book paid for a name nine years ago and is paying with hands now, and Iven reads the line and goes back down the spine without a word.
  - On the third morning no price is taken; Meren says in front of the crew that the line promised blood until the plant is still and was finished the moment the pen left the page, so the count had nothing to charge.
- decision: Whether the farm takes the officer's adult wording as its reading of the sign — answered by the crew the same morning, cutting (the boy from the water crew) or standing still (the woman from the second shift), and the reader decides what comes of a book that can always be paid.
- local_consequence: Rusk keeps his eye, the name request is closed on the page and not on the wall, the child's whole reading loses to an adult certainty, and the plant turns away from the crown lamps toward the trench.
- long_horizon: +1 year — an officer's wording precedes every reading on Hesper and the leaves go unread; +10 years — a farm that keeps the plant fed by hand on blood and warmth and no longer asks it anything; +100 years — a station whose only citizen is fed, obeyed and never heard again.
- payoff: thread-0005 — the count that stood at an eye is answered: the farm does not say the name, does not let the price be taken and does not close the book; it writes a wording it can always keep, and the book, not the child, decides what the sign says.
- return_hook: The open flower, which held its face to the two bowl shades since the lamps came on, keeps its face down toward the trench where the blood went in and does not come back to the light.
- new_entities: none new; the boy from the water crew and the woman from the second shift carry the episode.
- deferred_answers: Whether a line the farm can always keep ends a request in the law's own terms or only on the page, and what the count charges when a request is answered with a wording — due_chapter 7.
