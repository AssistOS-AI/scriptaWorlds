# Plan chapter 0007
- dramatic_question: When the officer writes an adult wording over the farm's new rule of choosing, what answers the line — the book, the plant, or the law?
- anchor_character: Rusk, officer of Hesper.
- character_want: To put the choosing back under the book's hand: a line the officer can write, read and answer, instead of a name that lives in one old woman's memory and nowhere in the farm's record.
- primary_idea: The page closes a count and never answers a plant: writing binds the farm's people and the hand that signed, while the plant answers only what a body does to it.
- human_need: Status and meaning — the officer's post, and a farm that can keep its own record of what it spends.
- opening_hook: Nine days after the flowering began the ring stands at the wall with a name given, a sign unchanged, and a plant that has not moved a leaf; Rusk opens the book and says the farm's choosing is a hole in its record.
- beats:
  - Day 16, before the first shift: Rusk argues that the rule of choosing made in the ring yesterday is a rule the book cannot carry, so the farm's seventh name exists only in Verrin's memory; Meren answers that the rule was made in front of the crew and does not need his pen.
  - Rusk writes his sixth line into the green journal — the name said at the roots is to be entered under the sign by the officer within the three days — and signs it.
  - Rusk goes down to the trench and asks Verrin for the name; she refuses to say it a second time, calls the line his debt and not hers, and leaves him with a line he cannot keep.
  - Day 17: nothing in the wall moves for a page held up to it; Meren offers to close the count by writing a new line over the same sign, as the officer did in the spring, and Rusk refuses to close the only record that a choosing happened.
  - Day 18, third morning: the count takes his left eye, the first eye ever paid on Hesper, and the count starts again on the same line.
  - Day 18, the trench: the book held where the flower is aimed moves nothing, and a quarter turn of the wheel at the root-line seat turns the low leaves, lifts the open flower off the trench for the first time in six days, and moves the gauge; Meren writes the number.
- decision: Rusk chooses between closing the count with a new line over the name sign and keeping both the line and the ladder running to an eye. The reader's agency is whether the farm closes the line, gives Verrin's name to the book, or gives the plant its water at the seat.
- local_consequence: The green journal carries a sixth line that no hand will keep; the officer of Hesper has one eye and three days; the work ring's gauge moves a tenth for the first time since the flowering began, and it moves for a wheel.
- long_horizon: At +1 year, the officer's post is known on Hesper as a post that spends a body for a record, and no one wants the pen; at +10 years, the line is still standing under the name sign and every officer after him inherits its count.
- payoff: The flower's aim is read at last (thread-0007): the plant has been attending to its own water at the seat where the root line enters the soil, and the farm's only instrument that moved moved for a quarter turn of a wheel. Also paid: whether a line the farm can always keep ends a request in the law's own terms or only on the page (def-0003) — it ends the count and never ends the asking.
- return_hook: The count restarts on the same unmet line, and nothing on Hesper knows what the ladder takes after an eye.
- new_entities: none.
- deferred_answers: What the law takes when the ladder has run out and the same unmet line is charged a fourth time — due_chapter 10.
