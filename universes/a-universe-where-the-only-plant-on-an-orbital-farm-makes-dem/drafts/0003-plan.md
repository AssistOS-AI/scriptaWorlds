# Plan chapter 0003

- dramatic_question: Can the officer's own wording answer a request the Reader can read but the farm cannot fulfil, and what does the plant do with the line he writes?
- anchor_character: Officer Rusk, sixty, the officer of Hesper, who writes the green journal and has one tooth and one finger less than he had a week ago.
- character_want: To write the farm's answer to the name request in the green journal himself, so that the book holds the farm's decision and not only the plant's question, and to say the forbidden name at the roots on the third morning.
- primary_idea: The green journal is the farm's instrument, not the plant's: the plant does not perceive writing at all, so a written answer changes nothing on the wall while the law still takes its price out of the writer.
- human_need: Authority: the need of a man who answers for a cost, and the need of a farm to have a mouth that can answer even when the answering costs a body.
- opening_hook: The name sign finishes coming up overnight; Bea reads it aloud as *give me a name*, and the farm has no dead child to give and no page in the book for its own answer.
- beats:
  1. Day 8: Bea reads the name sign at the roots as *give me a name*; the crew stops; Meren states in front of witnesses that no child has died on Hesper in nine years, so there is no name to give.
  2. Rusk fights the rule in front of the crew: the book can write what the plant wants and has no page for what the farm will do; the woman from the second shift refuses to be in the ring, the boy from the water crew says somebody has to answer, Meren puts the price of a second unmet line in the middle of the ring.
  3. Rusk writes the adult wording with his own name under it: the farm answers with the name it has, and the officer will say it at the roots on the third morning.
  4. Day 9: Iven comes up from the low ring to tell him the book is a chart and the plant will not notice; in the evening Bea holds the open journal up at the wall, gets nothing, and then holds up a hand lamp and the whole crown turns to it.
  5. Day 10: at the roots, in front of the crew, Rusk gets as far as the first sound of the name and cannot say it; the plant does not change by a stroke, the crown stays with the lamps and the gauge holds its number.
  6. Day 11: he wakes without the index finger of his left hand; the count restarts the same morning, the crew argues about closing the book, Meren refuses, and the farm is three days from an eye.
- decision: Whether a farm whose officer cannot carry out his own line should say the forbidden name, let the officer pay the next price, or stop writing in the green journal altogether.
- local_consequence: The officer loses a finger for a line the plant never read, the farm proves for itself that the plant is blind to writing, the crew's reading of the tooth is settled — the price follows the pen, not the plant's judgement — and a written answer that no one can carry out now stands three days from an eye.
- long_horizon: In a hundred years the journal is kept as a book about the crew rather than about the plant, and the post of the officer is defined by what he signs rather than by what the plant does.
- payoff: The crew settles what the officer's tooth proved: the law takes its price from the writer of an unmet line, in the order tooth, finger, eye, and the plant is neither offended nor listening.
- return_hook: The count has restarted on the name request, and the next price is an eye.
- new_entities: none; the chapter uses the existing crew (Rusk, Meren, Bea, Iven, the woman from the second shift, the boy from the water crew) and no new proper nouns.
- deferred_answers: none new; the naming of the flower and the child behind it keep their due date at chapter 4.
