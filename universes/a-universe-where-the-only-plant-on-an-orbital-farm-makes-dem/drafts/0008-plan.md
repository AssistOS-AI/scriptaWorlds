# Plan chapter 0008
- dramatic_question: When the officer writes the farm's seventh name into the green journal against the crew's own rule and takes the count off his own head, does the plant answer the writing?
- anchor_character: Rusk, officer of Hesper, one eye and three days.
- character_want: To close the count that runs toward an unknown fourth price and to put the farm's choosing into the book before the only head that holds the name is dead.
- primary_idea: A written name is a name the farm can read, and the law charges only lines that are not kept, so a farm rule no line carries costs nothing when it is broken.
- human_need: Meaning — a farm that can keep its own record of what it spends, against a record that outlives the truth of it.
- opening_hook: The nineteenth morning: the count restarted on the officer's sixth line, nothing on the ladder above an eye, and Rusk opens the book to write one more adult line over the same sign.
- beats:
  - Day 19, before the shift: Rusk tells the ring that a new line over the same sign takes the charge off the old one and does not strike the old line out, and that the new line will carry the name; the crew objects that the fifteenth day's rule was made at this wall, not by a pen.
  - Meren demands to read the name out loud for the farm; Rusk refuses, because a written name is a name nobody has to say, and Verrin answers from the rail that paper is the place where the farm's orders live.
  - Rusk writes the entry from his own hearing, the two sounds of the name inside it, signs it, and the count comes off the sixth line; Verrin refuses to read the page and tells him the farm will read in nine years what he wrote, with no hand left that heard it.
  - Day 20: the writing moves no leaf, and Meren opens the root line another quarter turn at the seat; the gauge moves a second tenth, so her column moves for a wheel and not for a page.
  - Day 21, third morning: no price is taken, and in the middle of that morning the flower closes and the wall lets its sign go leaf by leaf; the ring reads the quiet wall as the plant accepting the written name, and Meren and the child do not.
  - The ring goes back to work with a quiet wall and a name in the book that no living hand can confirm; Bea finds a small new shape come up in the leaves along the root line, smaller than any sign the farm knows.
- decision: The farm chooses between Meren's way (close the count over the sign and let the name stay unwritten), the water crew's way (feed the root line at the seat and trust the gauge), and Rusk's way (write the name into the book and let the count go). Rusk writes.
- local_consequence: The green journal carries the farm's seventh name in the officer's hand and the seventh line closes the sixth line's count, so the fourth price is never charged; the farm's first rule of choosing is broken without a price; the flowering ends, the sign over the shelf comes apart, and the wall is quiet for the first time in the book.
- long_horizon: At +9 years the next flowering reads the name off the page instead of out of a living memory, and no hand on the farm can say whether the officer heard it right; at +100 years the book's seventh name stands as the farm's only account of a child whose register page was cut, and the choosing is the officer's again.
- payoff: What the count does when the ladder is finished (thread-0009) — a line written over the same sign takes the charge off, so nothing is taken and the top of the ladder is never reached. Whether a name said once at the roots can be kept out of the farm's speech (def-0004) — the law has no price on a name, and the officer's pen put it on a page with nothing charged. Whether the name lives only in one memory (thread-0008) — the farm bought a page for it, and the page cannot be checked.
- return_hook: A new small shape stands in the leaves along the root line at the seat, and the Reader of Hesper has no entry for it.
- new_entities: none.
- deferred_answers: none new.
