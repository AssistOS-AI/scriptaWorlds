# Plan chapter 0009
- dramatic_question: With the wall quiet and the flowering over, who will hold the name the next flowering asks for, and who will have to say it out loud at the roots?
- anchor_character: Verrin, seventy-one, the oldest hand on Hesper, the only mouth that ever held a child for the plant.
- character_want: To be finished with the naming and to know that the farm is not carrying its whole memory of the dead in one old head that the next flowering will not have.
- primary_idea: A choosing needs a farm that remembers, and Hesper has spent its whole memory of dead children on this plant, so the choosing has to be made into a rule about who holds a name and for how long.
- human_need: Meaning — to belong to a place that can still say its own dead.
- opening_hook: The twenty-second morning: the wall is flat, the flower lies closed on the seat, there is no shape to read and no count to wait on, and the ring comes up to the root wall anyway.
- beats:
  - The quiet wall: Meren makes the root line the farm's first duty — the wheel read at the seat before every shift and the number written in her column — and Bea says the small shape at the water is not turned at the farm but at the water, so the plant is asking the ring for nothing.
  - Rusk opens the choosing while nothing is charged for it: the next sign will come in nine years and it will ask for a name, and today the farm holds two dead children in its mouths and both of them are already spent.
  - The ring counts its mouths at the wall: the register is read out, the surviving pages carry names no hand can put a face to, and the woman from the second shift says the only face this ring still holds is the one the law forbids it to name.
  - Two defensible answers are argued: the water crew's — a name off a page is still a name and the plant hears no words at all — against Meren's and Verrin's — a page gave the farm a name no mouth can check, and in nine years the page will be read by hands that never heard the child.
  - The ring makes the rule of the choosing in the open — at this wall, from children the ring knew alive, offered by a hand and never asked of one, with the ring free to refuse, and the name said once at the roots by that hand — and adds the keeping to it: one day a year at the wall for the farm's own dead, said from mouths and never from pages, whose first year has nothing to say because nine years without a funeral left the farm one name and the plant is holding it.
  - Verrin takes Bea into the trench and pays the last term of her own bargain: a name was spent at the roots, it is not in anything the child can read, and it will never be given to her; the child asks to be told the farm's dead from now on, and the ring makes her the first mouth of the keeping.
- decision: The farm chooses between reading the register's surviving names into the green journal and letting the officer's page hold the next name, and making a choosing that no page can carry: a hand that knew the child alive offers it, the ring may refuse the offer, and the keeper says the name once at the roots. The ring takes the second.
- local_consequence: Hesper has a rule of choosing it did not have, a yearly day for its dead whose first year is empty, and a child who has been told that a name was spent and will never be hers; the rule stands in no line of the green journal and the law charges nothing for it, and the woman from the second shift asks for the one name the fundamental law forbids the farm to say.
- long_horizon: At +9 years the next flowering asks for a name and the farm has nine years of its own dead in living mouths instead of one head and a page; at +100 years the keeping is the farm's calendar and its register is a book of holes nobody reads.

- return_hook: The woman from the second shift asks the wall that her daughter's name be one of the names said at the keeping — the one name the law of this universe has forbidden the farm to mention again.
- new_entities: none.
