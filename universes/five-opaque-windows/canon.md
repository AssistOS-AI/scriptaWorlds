# Canon — The Five Opaque Windows

## Fundamental laws
- Prohibition that holds in this world: no one was allowed to solve the problem before they could describe who would pay for the solution
- The world is built from the following speculative operations of the Periodic Table of Ideas:
- Finitely Extensible: a universe where the system remains finite yet extensible (science fantasy and meta-reality, reflexivity).
- Substrate-Independent Person: a universe where identity is a pattern rather than privileged material (mind and identity, outfinite).

These laws are inviolable. Every important event in the book must be a consequence of them, and exceptions
need a cause already established in canon.

Established consequences of the law, as they appear in the sector:
- The Ledger is the office that licenses a solution. It refuses a licence whose account names no payer, and refuses a payer whose price they do not bear and do not consent to in their own voice.
- A licensed solution is paid for by named people, not by the sector in general; each payer signs the licence themselves.
- The sector is finite and stays finite: the workshop has thirty-one rooms and always exactly thirty-one. What is new here is made by subtraction, never by addition; a quiet room is a room that was going to exist anyway, given to nobody.

## World
- The workshop is a children's sector, thirty-one rooms, kept by Odile, who reads almost every system under its roof: kettles, water lines, sleeping charts, ledgers, and the people, because people here are patterns and patterns are readable.
- Odile is not a god and does not claim to be; she built the sector and reads it from the outside.
- The workshop has five opaque windows, glazed in winter because the low-field light made the small ones squint at their work. The account for that work named the gardener of the water lines, who used to wave up at the children and lost the view.
- The quiet room is a region of a person's own pattern made unreadable to everybody: to Odile, to the other children, and to its owner. It is made by subtracting the record of an interval, not by building a lock.
- The price of a quiet room is paid twice by whoever hides: they keep the conclusion of the hidden interval and lose the reasoning that led to it, and they can never read back what they were doing.
- A quiet room is irreversible without the consent of the person hiding. Odile can undo any reading she built, and has said so.
- Aster, eleven, feels unreadable regions and other absences as physical sensation: a cold band, a draught along the neck, a stripped bed, a missing tooth found with the tongue.

## Recurring characters
- **Aster** — eleven, a student in the workshop. She feels absences physically. She reads everything out loud, including ledgers other people would keep quiet, and she does not experience reading as something that can be done discreetly. She wants the quarrel with Vela repaired before Vela leaves. In chapter 1 she predicted where the price of the quiet room would land, wrote her own name on the additional-payers line of the licence, and asked the others not to undo Vela's hour.
- **Vela** — older than Aster, a student in the workshop, fifth name on the transfer list to the upper workshop at the end of the season. She filed the quiet room licence, was refused once for naming Odile as the payer, then hid one hour of her own reasoning and signed as the payer. She apologised to Aster for the breakfast where Aster read the transfer list out loud, and cannot reconstruct why she chose the words she chose.
- **Odile** — the maker of the workshop, keeper of its systems. She reads almost everything; she is not a god and does not pretend to be. She sits on the floor to read. In chapter 1 she signed the quiet room licence as first payer, losing one student's hour out of her reading, and told the children she could undo it.

## Timeline
- Winter before chapter 1 — the five windows of the workshop are glazed opaque; the account names the gardener of the water lines.
- Spring before chapter 1 — Odile shows the children how a room is subtracted rather than built; the children begin four months of work on the quiet room.
- Morning of chapter 1 — Aster reads the transfer list aloud at breakfast; Vela's name is on it, fifth line, before Vela has told anyone; Vela leaves the table.
- Midday of chapter 1 — the Ledger sends back the quiet room licence for naming a payer who does not bear the price; Aster says the real price lands on her; Odile says her consent cannot yet be taken.
- Afternoon of chapter 1 — the licence is signed by Vela and Odile; Vela's hour of reasoning is hidden; she comes out with a conclusion and no reasons and apologises to Aster with words she cannot defend.
- Evening of chapter 1 — Aster writes her own name on the additional-payers line; the Ledger returns the licence flagged, calls her consent unverifiable, and schedules a review in three days.
- Chapter 1 + 3 days — the review of the quiet room account is due.

## Stable facts
- Nothing in the sector is hidden until the account is complete: the Ledger will not license a solution whose payers are unnamed.
- A payer's price must be describable before it is paid for their consent to count. Aster's price is not describable in advance, which is why her consent is flagged rather than accepted.
- Odile can read every system she built. She cannot read a subtracted interval; what she sees in its place is a duration with edges, an entry that says an hour happened and refuses to say what it was.
- Aster kept the workshop's five opaque windows as honest absences: everybody agreed to them and everybody can point at them.
- Vela's hidden hour remains hidden; nobody has undone it, and Aster and Vela both asked Odile not to.

## Mysteries with a fixed cause
- Why Aster feels unreadable regions as physical absence — cause fixed: Aster carries a small unreadable interval in her own pattern, subtracted from her record before the licence rule existed; the maker's audit holds it as a blank with a duration, and that is why Aster recognises that exact shape in other people. Hints so far: she is the one who said where the price of the quiet room would land, before anyone ran the test.
- What the review will do about a payer whose price cannot be named in advance — cause fixed: the Ledger has no wording for a price paid before it can be described, so the review will try to write one, and the wording it produces will become a rule other children are measured against. Hints so far: the flagged line on the licence, and the date set three days out.
