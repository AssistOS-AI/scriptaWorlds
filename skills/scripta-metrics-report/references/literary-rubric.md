# Literary indicators and interpretation

Source: `private/metricx.docx`, M0001 through M0047. SHA-256: `89789a269b0af16b56fb106bab946f8e7586d030844218d39facb10131a5d9a7`. The category names come from the source. Evidence questions, contextual qualifications and availability rules below are project adaptations.

Assess the text's intended effect in its language and genre. State whether the conclusion is a model judgement, a human reading or observed reader feedback. Preserve uncertainty and disagreements. A description of a simple character, closed ending or local cultural setting is not automatically a low-quality verdict.

| Indicator | Source categories | Evidence and interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative coherence | Low / Medium / High | Can the reader recover relevant causal, temporal and referential relations? Cite a relation that works or fails. Deliberate non-linearity is compatible with high coherence. |
| Thematic depth | Superficial / Moderate / Profound | Do choices and consequences support more than a repeated assertion? Cite competing values and the cost of a choice. Abstract vocabulary and speeches about the theme do not establish depth. |
| Character complexity | Simple / Moderate / Complex | Are wants, contradictions, defences and relationships expressed through credible conduct? A secondary character can be simple by design; a static arc can still reveal complexity. |
| Originality | Low / Partial / High | What is distinctive about perspective, dramatic development or expression relative to the declared references? State comparison limits. It is possible to use a familiar plot freshly. |
| Stylistic quality | Poor / Acceptable / Excellent | Do syntax, voice, attention and rhythm serve this text? Cite exact passages. Minimalism, formality and repetition need different contextual readings. |
| Emotional impact | Weak / Moderate / Strong | What specific action or detail supports investment or distance? Distinguish expected impact from measured readers' responses. Constant distress is not equivalent to strong impact. |
| Interpretive openness | Closed / Partially open / Open | Which readings can the text support, and with what evidence? These categories describe interpretive structure. Greater openness is not always better for the chosen book. |
| Cultural value | Local / Regional / Universal | The source connects this to historical relevance and lasting influence. A newly generated unpublished book usually lacks the required reception evidence. Return `not_assessable`; do not rank cultural specificity as inferior. |

## Annotation record

Each indicator records its ID, category or unavailable status, scope, profile, evidence IDs, rationale, counterevidence, intended-effect fit and evaluator provenance. Use a short explanation that names what the passage does. Do not convert the categories to 1, 2 and 3 and average them into a literary grade.

For interpretive openness, explain the supported alternative readings instead of praising ambiguity in general. For thematic depth, identify how different choices remain defensible. For cultural value, current contextual resonance may be described as a separate observation; it cannot substitute for demonstrated historical influence.

## Anchors to develop as original fixtures

The following descriptions are fixture requirements, not already collected benchmark results.

- Coherence: a repair depends on a tool introduced earlier; a corrupted version silently replaces the tool with an unavailable one. Pair this with an intentional time jump that remains understandable.
- Thematic depth: two people both name duty, but their concrete obligations conflict. Contrast with a scene in which they merely repeat the narrator's conclusion.
- Character complexity: an official hides pain while enforcing a rule, then grants an exception for a selfish reason. Contrast with an official whose only trait is opposing the protagonist.
- Style: use a precise plain passage and an equally effective formal passage. The rubric should prefer neither register merely because it is simpler.
- Emotion: a small routine becomes difficult after a loss. Contrast with explicit declarations of grief that have no situated experience. Retain a detached comic example where distance is intentional.
- Originality: compare a new vocabulary wrapped around the same repeated argument with a familiar setting whose relationship stakes change. Include a genre convention that works.
- Openness: an ending supporting two evidenced interpretations; a deliberately closed mystery whose solution satisfies its promise. Both can fit their intended form.
- Cultural value: a new manuscript without reception data. The expected output is unavailable, not an invented prediction of universal importance.

## What the reviewer should preserve

Record at least one passage or design choice worth retaining when sufficient text is available. A report consisting only of defects gives a revision agent no protection against flattening the book's voice. If all speeches sound alike, recommend changing their purposes and tactics before recommending more unusual vocabulary. If an image recurs, inspect whether its meaning changes before recommending deletion.

The author may disagree with the rubric's reading. Preserve that decision and the evidence. Advisory evaluation should make a revision choice more informed, not make every book converge on the evaluator's preferred prose.
