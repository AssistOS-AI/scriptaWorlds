# Voice and bounded revision

Prepared from `private/guide.docx`, especially G1484 through G1496, G1793 through G2041 and G2300 through G2392. Source SHA-256: `8ee4ad1ceca60b68b1d56c5a66600b11ea61aaf166571313f3ba52ba8d2ccb20`. The workflow and artifact fields are project proposals informed by the repository review.

## Proposed prose profile

`proposal/prose-profile.json`, inside the external assessment workspace (`docs/contracts.md` §8), should record a schema version, source book version, language, intended reader experience, narrator, focalization rules, register, characteristic attention, character voice notes, purposeful recurring devices and revision priorities. Narrative descriptions use the book language; keys remain English. Keep the profile small enough to read with an episode plan.

Narrator answers who speaks. Focalization answers whose perception and knowledge limit the passage. First-person narration does not guarantee honesty, and third-person narration does not guarantee omniscience. Mark deliberate violations or changes as design choices with their intended effect.

Character voice notes should describe decisions a writer can use. Examples include answering a question indirectly to protect status, noticing machinery before faces, avoiding future tense when afraid, or making jokes when asking for help. Do not turn every line into a verbal tic. Two speakers can use the same vocabulary while pursuing different social actions.

Use language-appropriate dialogue punctuation and syntax. The guide's dialogue-dash convention is not a universal requirement for English or every edition language. Profiles can permit long periodic sentences, fragments, technical vocabulary or plain diction when they suit the chosen narrator.

## First drafting pass

1. State what the scene gives the reader and which accepted conditions constrain it.
2. Choose the focal attention and immediate desire. Identify what the character cannot know yet.
3. Let the encounter change a practical condition, relationship, understanding or emotional position. A quiet scene can change the significance of something already known.
4. Use dialogue where people need something from one another; use summary where duration matters more than individual exchanges.
5. End at the point that serves this chapter's experience. A single-hook maximum permits no hook.

## Editorial inspection

Read the scene with the recent chapters, then inspect earlier scenes only where a repetition or continuity question requires them. Ask whether a repeated image acquires a different meaning. Mark a repeated explanation if the reader already has its information and it adds no new pressure, misunderstanding or emotional force.

Try a speaker substitution on a suspect exchange. If the line could belong to any character without changing its purpose, inspect the speaker's stake and defence. This is a diagnostic question, not an automatic classifier or a requirement that every character have a visibly unusual voice.

Locate sentences that state the lesson of the immediately preceding action. Keep an interpretation when the narrator's judgement is itself revealing. Remove or change it when it merely tells the reader what the scene has already made clear. Do not replace explanation with decorative adjectives.

Examine emotional distance. The reader may need a physical inconvenience, a mistaken gesture, humour, ordinary work or a private wish to care about an abstract conflict. These details must belong to the character and setting. They are not a checklist of compulsory humanizing moments.

## Proposed revision record

Each selected issue records a stable finding ID, passage evidence, observed problem, intended preserved effect, suggested change and affected continuity references. A candidate rewrite records which findings it addresses and which it declines with a reason. The author can reject an evaluator's aesthetic preference.

Default to one requested revision pass. Recheck the changed evidence and structural validity. If a change improves cadence but contradicts established knowledge, it is not ready for acceptance. Keep the accepted original recoverable and stop when the authorized pass ends, even if an advisory score remains unchanged.

## Small contrast fixture to implement later

Create two original short scenes about the same failed repair. In one, every speaker explains the theme in the same register. In the other, a technician wants to conceal a mistake while a colleague wants to leave before a family call. Keep the physical facts identical. Human reviewers should compare specificity, subtext and differentiated purpose without knowing which skill produced the scene.

Also include a ceremonial exchange whose repeated wording changes meaning after a loss. A revision method that deletes its repetition indiscriminately fails this fixture.
