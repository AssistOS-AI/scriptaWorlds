# Narrative blocks and rhythm

Prepared from `private/guide.docx`, G1470 through G2306. Source SHA-256: `8ee4ad1ceca60b68b1d56c5a66600b11ea61aaf166571313f3ba52ba8d2ccb20`. The classifications below adapt the guide for authoring and annotation. They do not prescribe proportions.

## Units and labels

| Layer | Units or modes | What to decide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural | Scene, sequence, chapter | Where an experience forms a unit and how it contributes to a larger movement |
| Content | Action, conflict, event | What happens, what opposes a want and what changes |
| Expression | Description, dialogue, narration, interior monologue | How the reader receives the experience |
| Rhythm | Suspense, cliffhanger, pause, acceleration | How disclosure, duration and expectation shape attention |

These layers overlap. A scene can contain dialogue, description and interior thought while sustaining suspense. A chapter may contain several scenes or one extended scene. A sequence can cross a chapter boundary. A pause can alter a relationship through attention without introducing a new event. An annotation system must allow these combinations.

A scene has a situated experience, a perceiving or organizing perspective and a reason to occupy narrative space. The change can concern action, knowledge, emotion, a relationship or the reader's understanding. Requiring a new crisis in every scene would erase useful forms of stillness.

A sequence groups related movement toward a local purpose. A chapter is also a reading unit: its boundary controls interruption and expectation. It need not end on a cliffhanger. A complete local gesture, an unresolved silence or a changed understanding can justify the boundary.

## Expression choices

Description establishes space, sensory experience and the significance of a detail to the person noticing it. Select details through attention. Someone hiding an injury may notice the weight of a sleeve before the room's colour. Longer description is justified when its duration matters to the reading experience.

Dialogue is action between people. Identify what a speaker is trying to obtain, avoid or conceal. Replies can resist, bargain, redirect or misunderstand. Information delivery alone rarely explains why two people need to speak now. Silence and unfinished replies can carry meaning if the surrounding action makes that meaning available.

Narration can compress routine, connect events, establish distance or offer a narrator's interpretation. Summary is useful when the skipped duration is less important than its consequence. Dramatizing every meal and journey can flatten rhythm as much as summarizing every confrontation.

Interior monologue gives access to subjective thought, including association and contradiction. Its syntax and reliability depend on the chosen voice. Do not require every thought to explain motivation correctly. People can misread themselves.

## Rhythm choices

Suspense depends on meaningful uncertainty and the reader's investment. A cliffhanger suspends a consequential moment at a boundary. Neither requires withholding an answer that is already obvious to the viewpoint character solely to manipulate the reader.

A pause gives attention to an image, thought or relation while event time slows. Acceleration compresses duration or increases the rate of consequential changes. Short sentences can contribute to either tension or flatness; sentence length alone is not a pacing measure.

Alternation is a response to the scene's purpose. A dialogue-heavy negotiation and a nearly wordless aftermath can both work. A succession of chapters with the same public argument, explanation and settlement deserves review because its dramatic movement repeats, even when its vocabulary changes.

## Annotation proposal

Later annotations should record `segment_id`, evidence boundaries, structural membership, expressive modes, focalization, local purpose and observed change. Rhythm labels include a textual rationale. Planned labels and inferred labels have different provenance. No fixed percentage of modes is required, and an uncertain scene boundary stays marked as inferred.

Use distributions and repeated sequences as prompts for editorial inspection. They are not independent proof that the book is monotonous or successful.
