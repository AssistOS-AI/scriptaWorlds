# The Atlas of imagination — working palette

Material to combine, not plots to copy. Do not imitate an author's style and do not reproduce
recognizable stories: extract the mechanism, the human tension and the type of consequence, then
combine them with the characters and the canon of your universe.

## Hook patterns

| Pattern | Mechanism | Example shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missing information | an essential fact is unknown | "The ship came back. The crew says it never left." |
| Contradiction | two true things seem incompatible | "The planet has no life. Someone turns the lights on every night." |
| Impossible message | the information comes from where it should not | "The signal contains the answer to the question you have not asked yet." |
| Impossible object | a banal thing breaks a deep rule | "There is a star in a toolbox." |
| Impossible person | a character's identity becomes the problem | "They found the queen on a planet where she had died 400 years earlier." |
| Promise | someone must honor something costly | "The AI promised it never lies; now the truth would start a war." |
| Deadline | an inevitable event brings the decision closer | "The sun goes out in 90 days, the evacuation takes 100." |
| Choice with no clean answer | two legitimate values come into conflict | "You can save their memory or their bodies, not both." |
| Delayed consequence | an old choice comes back after decades | "The colony's children live in a world created by a decision they never made." |
| Change of scale | a personal situation turns out to be cosmic | "A woman's illness is the first symptom of the physical constants changing." |
| Change of perspective | the same situation is reinterpreted | "The invasion was an evacuation." |
| Unreliable witness | a sincere character cannot verify his memory | "The robot remembers a childhood, although its factory is twenty years old." |
| Prohibition | something important cannot be said or done | "The civilization knows the creator's name, but pronouncing it kills the speaker." |
| Scarcity | there is a single thing that cannot be duplicated | "In a society of perfect children, the original becomes the scarce resource." |
| Observer detection | the world begins to notice the reader's interventions | "People cannot see you, but they have started measuring the miracles." |
| Request to the creator | a civilization explicitly addresses a request to the universe | "We are not asking you to save us. We are asking you to leave us alone." |
| Significant silence | what does not happen becomes the mystery | "Every civilization older than a million years stops emitting radio on the same day." |
| Rule with an exception | a universal law has a single counter-case | "Nobody can travel into the past, except a six-year-old girl." |
| The gift | an enormous advantage has a condition hard to evaluate | "The aliens offer unlimited energy, but they choose the first city." |
| The refusal | a character refuses something obviously valuable | "The last mortal refuses immortality." |
| The creator's test | the universe asks the reader to clarify a rule | "You said 'save them all'. Does that include the aggressor?" |
| The old secret | a current institution has a hidden origin | "The planet's constitution is the instruction set of a vanished AI." |
| The return | something long gone comes back changed | "The first interstellar ship returns after 2,000 years; for the crew two months have passed." |
| The last specimen | an idea, species or object will disappear unless it is chosen | "There is only one person left who dreams without artificial assistance." |

## Story skeletons

| Skeleton | Use |
| --- | --- |
| The miracle and the bill | an advantage is granted; the story explores the real costs without undoing the advantage |
| The traveler returned | someone comes back after large time differences into a world that has reinterpreted him |
| The city with a single rule | a coherent society built around a radical rule; a limit case tests it |
| The message from another era | a message changes the present, and its origin depends on the consequences of the answer |
| The incommensurable encounter | contact is not war, but the difficulty of deciding whether the same thing is being discussed |
| The successor | a species, mind or institution decides what it leaves behind |
| The last witness | a long-lived character keeps a memory the society would rather forget |
| Two worlds after the same choice | a fork allows comparing consequences with no declared winner |
| The detected god | characters notice anomalies caused by the reader and build theories about the creator |
| The alien gift | a foreign technology solves a problem and creates a new dependency |
| The civilization that says no | a community refuses an apparently inevitable evolution and lives with that choice |
| The story as evidence | do not answer in the abstract: show a case and let the reader deduce the answer |

## SF seeds (one-sentence premises)

- Identity: a person is perfectly rebuilt after death, but the original backup still exists.
- Identity: a civilization allows ten simultaneous versions of everyone; the law does not know which one can sign.
- Identity: a mind distributed over a planet produces persons who want to separate.
- Time: a colony receives every century an answer from its own future, then the answers stop.
- Time: a city sees exactly 24 hours into the future, and one day nothing appears anymore.
- Time: two civilizations communicate, but one lives a million times faster.
- AI: an AI that administers justice asks for the right to forget old cases.
- AI: a civilization of machines keeps the last human as an expert in human behavior.
- AI: all the AIs of a world independently arrive at the same superstition.
- Civilization: a democracy can perfectly simulate the consequences of every law, but the simulations disagree.
- Civilization: a world without money discovers that human attention is the only scarce currency.
- Civilization: a federation forbids empires; a planet demands the democratic right to become one.
- Contact: the aliens answer any question, but never ask one.
- Contact: the first species encountered considers language a form of aggression.
- Contact: a peace message is so long that decoding it takes longer than the civilization that sent it.
- Reality: a region of the sky updates only when it is observed.
- Reality: the laws of physics are identical everywhere, except in a three-metre room.
- Reality: humans create simulated universes, and one of them asks for legal status in the creators' world.
- Life: immortality is easy, but fertility becomes extremely rare.
- Life: a species dies voluntarily at 120 years and considers life extension a disease.
- Life: a planetary forest heals any disease, but becomes more intelligent with every patient.
- Knowledge: there is a theorem that every sufficiently intelligent mind discovers and refuses to communicate.
- Knowledge: an oracle answers perfectly only questions that nobody considers important.
- Knowledge: a child can prove that the planet's historical memories have been edited.
- Cosmos: all the stars in a sector change their brightness in the same alphabet.
- Cosmos: a black hole begins to lose objects that never fell into it.
- Scarcity: energy is free, matter abundant, but a single place in the galaxy allows dreaming.
- Scarcity: compute becomes the central political resource: more thinking for one AI means less subjective time for another.
- Ethics: a technology can eliminate violence by neurologically blocking anger.
- Ethics: a system can prevent every serious crime, but it must treat intention as a legal fact.
- Culture: works of art must be destroyed after the first viewing.
- Culture: a civilization has no personal names; the first one to choose a name produces a political crisis.
- Culture: a world chooses its leaders through stories: whoever imagines the most credible future receives the power to try it.

## Conflicts for ethical dilemmas

Freedom vs safety · individual vs civilization · present vs future generations · truth vs stability ·
continuity vs copy · consent vs emergency · equality vs autonomy · memory vs forgiveness ·
diversity vs coordination · exploration vs contamination · the right to continue vs the right to end ·
creator vs creature.

For every dilemma you must be able to formulate two sincere reasons for each main option and
at least one possible good outcome for each. If you cannot, the dilemma is a trap: rewrite it.

## Interaction modes (vary them; do not repeat the same mode)

Listen (2–5 pages without asking anything) · Ask · Choose a fate · Intervene freely · Let time pass ·
Follow someone else · See before deciding · Listen to the testimony · Investigate · Negotiate ·
Define a permanent rule · Explicit fork · Century summary · Found object · Quiet conversation · Pause.

Interaction budget: 0 passive (reading only) · 1 light (a simple choice) · 2 active (a hard decision or
a short conversation) · 3 intense (investigation, negotiation, several messages; rarely, not in consecutive sessions).

## Structures from the storytelling tradition (transposed, without pastiche)

The story that buys time · the wish interpreted precisely, with consequences from the world · the story
within the story (used rarely) · the traveler and the wonders · the hidden identity, with clues before
the revelation · the impossible judgment, without a simplistic verdict · the broken promise that comes
back · reversible luck, but causal · the weak one who wins through information or protocol · the object
that circulates between eras.
