## Meaning and the reader's promise

A central idea is the situation or possibility that can sustain the story's inquiry. A premise makes that possibility actionable by connecting a person, a want, resistance and consequences. "A plant demands sacrifices" is an idea. A caregiver who must decide whose treatment to interrupt because of a demand supplies a dramatic premise.

A topic names a subject such as duty. A thematic question asks what the story will examine, such as whether care remains care when it is imposed. A thematic statement is one possible answer. Keep the question open during drafting; characters can reach incompatible answers. The guide's discussion of wisdom can inform an earned insight without requiring a universal lesson at every chapter ending.

The reader promise names an expected experience and a meaningful uncertainty. Genre conventions help establish that promise. They are options and expectations, not a fixed event checklist. A speculative book can promise wonder, dread, social observation or intimacy in different proportions. Record a deliberate change to that promise when the accepted story supports it.

## Organization and design artifacts

| Term | Working definition | Implementation consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative model | Abstract pattern of transformation or persistence | Optional choice; static, circular and fragmented forms remain possible |
| Narrative structure | Arrangement of events and their disclosure | Track story time separately from order of presentation |
| Plot element or device | A means of changing or disclosing a situation | Use because it serves the scene, not to fill every blueprint slot |
| Blueprint | Concrete but revisable plan of arcs, chapters and scenes | Future plans remain proposals |
| Architecture | Relationships among plot, characters, theme, world and rhythm | Review whether the parts support one another |
| Construction system | Workflow that generates, verifies and revises those artifacts | Separate authoring from acceptance and assessment |

The guide uses some of these terms at overlapping levels. These working definitions keep schemas understandable; they do not claim that literary theory has one universal hierarchy. A sequence can cross chapters. One scene can contribute to several arcs. A deliberate gap in chronology is not automatically a causal error.

## Characters and relationships

A useful character record explains choices under pressure. Capture a present want, what the character fears losing, a habitual defence, an internal contradiction and the limits of their knowledge. Add a private concern unrelated to solving the speculative premise. Record a few specific speech and attention habits without writing a psychological essay.

Distinguish stable attributes, current conditions and developments. A lost tooth, a changed belief and a promotion have different persistence. A positive, negative or static arc can work. A stable character can expose a changing institution. A contradiction between belief and conduct can be the point of a scene.

Track relationships directionally. One person can trust another while the other resents the obligation. A relationship entry needs participants, current terms, last meaningful change, source evidence and an unresolved pressure. Categories such as rivalry, kinship, mentorship or intimacy are descriptive aids. They do not determine the next scene.

In the reviewed book, characters often return to interpreting the same public rule. A useful design question is what each person wants when the rule is not being debated. A parent may need privacy, an officer may hide pain, and a child may want someone to play unfairly in their favour. These possibilities are examples, not new facts about the existing book.

## World assumptions and consequences

Worldbuilding connects material conditions with institutions and daily life. Consider geography, resources, biology, time, technology, social organization, economy, politics, customs and shared beliefs when they affect a character's choices. Do not add an encyclopaedia merely because a category exists.

Separate permanent law, established observed effect, character belief, institutional rule and unresolved hypothesis. A crew voting on a naming procedure establishes a social rule. It does not establish how a plant physically recognizes a name. A reported divine demand can remain uncertain, provided the narration does not silently promote a belief into objective physics.

For each new exception to a cost or limitation, identify the earlier evidence that permits it and the consequence of using it. If that evidence does not exist, propose a discovery with its own uncertainty. Do not record a convenient exception as if it had always been known.
